I spent three hours formatting a resume in Google Docs once. The bullet points wouldn't align. A new section threw off the margins. I rage-quit and started looking for alternatives.

That frustration is why resume.craftisle.com exists.

## What it is

The Resume Builder is based on Reactive Resume — an open-source resume builder. Craftisle's version strips the account requirement. All data stays in your browser's localStorage. No signup, no cloud sync, no resume database someone else can hack.

## What you can do

**Rich text editor** with 15+ section types: summary, experience, education, skills, projects, certifications, languages, interests, references, custom sections. Bold, italic, links, bullet lists — standard formatting.

**3-4 templates** — clean, ATS-friendly designs. No elaborate infographic-style templates. Those look nice but get mangled by applicant tracking systems. Every template exports clean, parseable text.

**Export to PDF and DOCX**. PDF uses the browser's print engine — open the preview, save as PDF. DOCX export generates an editable Word document.

**Dark/light mode** — cosmetic but nice for late-night editing.

## What it's not

- **Not a design tool.** If you want Canva-style visual resumes with custom fonts and graphics, use Canva. This builder prioritizes ATS readability over visual flair.
- **No cloud backup.** Your resume lives in localStorage. Clear your browser data, lose your resume. Export frequently.
- **No AI writing.** No GPT-generated bullet points. No keyword optimization. You write the content.

## Why local storage matters

If you upload your resume to a "free" builder that stores it on their servers, you're trusting them with your full work history, education, and contact info. If they get breached, that data is public. With localStorage, your data never leaves your machine.

The trade-off: you're responsible for backups. Export a PDF and DOCX after each edit.
