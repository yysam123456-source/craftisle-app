# ID Photo Maker: Passport & Visa Photo Sizes Explained (Free, 2026)

An ID photo has two jobs: the right size, and the right appearance. The size part is mechanical and a tool handles it perfectly. The appearance part is where most applications actually fail — and it is worth knowing the difference before you submit.

This guide covers the exact dimensions the [ID photo maker](/tools/id-photo) supports, what to check before uploading, and where the tool's responsibility ends and yours begins.

## The six supported sizes

The tool produces these standard formats, each defined in both pixels and millimetres:

| Size | Pixels | Millimetres | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Inch** | 295 × 413 | 25 × 35 mm | Chinese ID documents, forms, CVs |
| **2 Inch** | 413 × 579 | 35 × 49 mm | Chinese official documents, applications |
| **Passport** | 330 × 453 | 35 × 45 mm | Passport and general international ID |
| **US Visa** | 600 × 600 | 51 × 51 mm | United States visa and passport |
| **UK Visa** | 350 × 450 | 35 × 45 mm | United Kingdom visa |
| **Schengen** | 350 × 450 | 35 × 45 mm | Schengen visa applications |

Pixel dimensions and millimetre dimensions refer to the same physical size at different print resolutions — pixels govern the digital file, millimetres govern the printed result. **Requirements change, and individual authorities can be stricter than the general standard**, so confirm the current specification with whoever is processing your application. The tool applies these dimensions accurately; it cannot verify that a given authority still accepts them. For background on how these standards vary internationally, see [passport photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport_photo) on Wikipedia.

## Background colour is a rule, not a preference

Most ID photo rejections that are not about the face itself come down to the background. Authorities specify it precisely — usually plain white, light blue or red depending on the document and country, with no shadows, gradients or objects.

The tool generates each of these colours cleanly by separating the subject from the original background and compositing onto a flat field. That is a different operation from painting a coloured rectangle behind a photo; the result has properly cut edges rather than a visible outline where the subject meets the background.

## Getting the source photo right

The tool can only work with what it is given, so the source photo determines most of the outcome.

**Shoot slightly wider than the final frame.** The output is a tight head-and-shoulders crop, and a photo taken exactly at head-and-shoulders framing leaves nothing to crop. A little margin on all sides gives the process room to position the crop correctly.

**Face the camera squarely, with even lighting.** Strong side lighting creates shadows the background removal has to interpret, and a tilted head is difficult to correct after the fact.

**Keep a neutral expression and open eyes**, with the face unobstructed — no hats, and glasses are frequently disallowed or must be free of glare.

**Use the highest resolution you have.** The output is small by design, but a higher-resolution source gives the crop and background separation more detail to work with.

## What the tool does, and what it cannot do

Understanding the boundary prevents surprises:

**Handled automatically** — separating the subject from the original background, compositing onto the required flat colour, resizing to the exact pixel dimensions of the chosen format, and producing a downloadable file.

**Not handled** — whether your expression, head angle, lighting, clothing or eyewear meet a specific authority's rules. Those are judgement calls made by a human reviewer, and no tool can certify them.

**A practical check before printing:** verify the head occupies roughly the share of the frame the authority expects, that the background is uniform with no gradient or shadow, and that nothing in the image is cropped awkwardly at the edges.

## Printing and file formats

The generated file is a standard JPEG sized for the chosen format. For printing, most photo shops accept the digital file directly and reproduce it at the correct physical dimensions; if you are printing at home, set the print size to the millimetre figure rather than letting the software fit to page.

Because the output size is small, keep the original photo as well — it is useful if you later need a different format or a larger print.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a selfie?**
Yes, if it meets the appearance requirements: shot straight on, evenly lit, with the face clear and unobstructed. Phone cameras are more than adequate at the resolution these photos need. What matters is the framing and the lighting, not the device.

**Is this photo guaranteed to be accepted?**
No. The tool applies the correct dimensions and produces the specified background, which covers the mechanical requirements. Lighting, expression, head position and the authority's own current rules are outside what any tool can guarantee. Check the receiving body's specification before relying on the result.

**What if my document needs a size not listed?**
The six supported formats cover most common requirements. Where an authority specifies something different, the closest match resized to the exact required pixel dimensions usually works — but verify the numbers yourself rather than assuming.

**Is my photo uploaded when I use this?**
No. The background removal and resizing both run in your browser. That matters here more than for most tools: an ID photo is exactly the kind of image you would not want sitting on someone else's server.

**Why does the photo look like it has a slightly soft edge?**
That is the subject being cut out and composited onto a flat colour. A hard, aliased edge would look worse; the soft boundary is what makes the result read as a single photograph rather than a cut-out pasted onto a background.
